## Design Context

### Users and use

The creator explicitly asks for an English-speaking learner, illustrations,
ancient Greek and Latin, unusual ancient mathematics, and work that teaches
something consequential. They reject generic catalogues and superficial demos.
Here the learner must work out a single hanging position that balances every
cross-section, then distinguish that discovery from a proof of a solid's volume.

### Personality and direction

Chosen for this project: a spare mathematical cabinet, with a large working
balance above a carefully annotated manuscript reading. Warm ivory, black olive
ink, brick red for the sphere, ochre for the cone, and dark green for the
cylinder. Restrained rules, generous margins, serif propositions and humanist
control text. No decorative antiquarian texture and no dashboard cards.

### Principles

1. Area and lever arm must both be visible; a level bar alone teaches too little.
2. Use the source's large auxiliary cylinder, and distinguish the smaller one.
3. A finite stack is a modern approximation, with visible numerical error.
4. Preserve editorial uncertainty and the source's conflicting number words.
5. Every drag has a native keyboard control; a phone gets an upright composition.
6. Saved studies retain the selected experiment, complete readings and sources.
