ADAPTATIONS
Ten selected Greek excerpts use the expanded side of TEI choice, join split
words and omit marginal/superscript markers and figure labels. Supplied letters have red
angle brackets, uncertain readings dotted underlines and expanded abbreviation
letters italic. Word spacing, accents and punctuation are editorial. The reading
does not silently correct the earlier 'twice' to the later 'four times'.

Six images are full-frame JPEG reductions of the project's registered TIFFs:
Sinar_true_pack8 and Sinar_pseudo_no-veil for the three folios. images.json
records original URLs, TIFF hashes, dimensions and exact reduction parameters.
Selection rectangles reuse the project's line coordinates. They are not new
recognition, retouched letters, or claims of an independently deciphered reading.
The visible composite and processed image are distinct supplied project products.

New English translations, Latin study renderings, language notes and teaching
prose: Copyright (c) 2026 Anthony Mercer, CC BY 3.0. The Latin was newly composed
for this workshop in 2026; it is not an ancient witness or Heiberg's translation.
Translations are teaching aids and have not received independent philological
peer review. Attribute the project and the source contributors when adapting.

MATHEMATICAL CROSS-CHECK
T. L. Heath, The Method of Archimedes, recently discovered by Heiberg (Cambridge,
1912), proposition 2, printed pp. 18-21. Public-domain English translation.
https://www.wilbourhall.org/pdfs/archimedes/archimedesHeiberg.pdf
Our new English and Latin are not presented as Heath's words. The source labels
follow the Greek transcription, including Theta (Theta is often H in redrawings).

PARABOLA WORKSHOP
Complete printed pages from Heiberg 1881 and the Harvard-hosted Method excerpt
(Heiberg 1913), public domain. The new readings use this print, not new
manuscript decipherment. The English, notes, code and diagrams in this workshop
are MIT, Anthony Mercer. The source manifest retains the original PDF and
derived whole-page identities. No modern third-party illustrations are used.
